The fourth discipline

Beyond the disciplines

The fourth discipline in triathlon is everything around the three sports: fitting them into everyday life and staying injury-free while you do it.

It takes time to learn to listen to your body. That is, in the end, the biggest benefit of the sport next to getting fitter: you develop a feel for how you react to food, rest and training load. Too much volume, too little sleep, too few carbs — and training falls apart.

The big advantage of triathlon is that you train several sports, which avoids the one-sided load of, say, marathon training. But stable muscles, good habits and sensible kit matter just as much as the endurance work.

Body

Look after your body

Strength training

Strong, stable muscles protect you from injury and help your long-term health. You will be surprised what 30–60 minutes of heavy strength work once or twice a week does. Go for big exercises that use many muscles, like pull-ups and squats.

Mobility & stretching

Often neglected, but it helps prevent injuries and takes only 5–10 minutes. Loosening your thighs with a foam roller is a good start.

Nutrition

Everyone has to find out what works for them. As a rule: carbohydrate-rich food two to three hours before training gives you fuel, and enough protein supports recovery.

Recovery

The real adaptations happen while you rest. Easy days and enough food are part of the plan — schedule them like you schedule sessions.

Sleep & stress

Exams, deadlines and training all draw from the same energy. In busy weeks, shorten a session instead of skipping sleep. A calm week of training beats a heroic one that leaves you ill.

Pain & injuries

Muscle soreness is normal; sharp or lasting pain is not. If something hurts for more than a few days, rest and talk to a physio or doctor instead of training through it.

Training

Train smart

Know-how

What is this Zone 2 everyone talks about? To get better at endurance sport you cannot avoid some training theory. The most common mistake is overtraining: train smarter, not harder. A smartwatch with heart-rate tracking helps you judge your load — but do not cling to theory.

Transitions & brick sessions

The change between disciplines has its own rules in a race, and your body has to get used to running after a long time in the saddle. Brick sessions combine both.

Organisation

Consistency beats heroics. Plan your week, protect your sessions and keep your kit ready. A good routine is what lets you train regularly next to your studies.

Listen to your body

Find the right mix of discipline and listening. In the end you only stick with it if training is fun.

Gear

Triathlon-specific gear

Three disciplines mean three sets of equipment — but you can start cheaply. A decent pair of running shoes, swimwear and goggles are enough at the start. You do not even need a bike: join our spinning sessions or hop on a bike at your gym. Later, the room for optimisation is limitless — but it does not have to be an expensive TT bike. These are the pieces that are specific to triathlon:

Tri suit

In training you practise the disciplines one at a time. On race day you want one suit for all three. A tri suit has a thin pad that adds comfort on the bike without getting in the way when you run.

Wetsuit

As soon as you start open-water swimming, a wetsuit is highly recommended. It keeps you warm and adds buoyancy, and together with a tow float you are much safer. Choose a swim-specific wetsuit, not a surf suit, ideally around 4–5 mm thick.

TT bike & aero kit

In most races you ride on your own, so you cannot use the slipstream of a group and ride in an aero position. A dedicated aero bike and helmet make you even faster. To start, a normal road bike is enough — or you ride indoors.

Heart-rate monitor

Optional, but very useful for judging your training load. A smartwatch is the most practical option.

Second-hand is your friend

You can find real bargains on Vinted and similar platforms: cycling kit, tri suits, wetsuits, even bikes. Look for good brands, good condition and items that are not too old, and you can start very cheaply. If you have kit to pass on, or want to hear about second-hand offers in the club, let us know.
